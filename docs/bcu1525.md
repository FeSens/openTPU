# The BCU1525 (Virtex UltraScale+ VU9P, 4 x DDR4, PCIe Gen3 x16)

The second board target (docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-multi-target-design.md, D8). This page
is the board's reference: what is known about it, the design choices, the pins, the LiteDRAM
images and their flow, the DDR4 bring-up, the board management controller, the thermal governor
and the fan. Much of it was written before the hardware existed and is kept as the record of
what was checked offline; where the card later confirmed or changed something, the section says
so.

**Status.** Two cards run, each with four 8 GB DDR4-2400 UDIMMs.

LiteX's `sqrl_xcu1525` platform (litex-boards, BSD-2) has the same pins but describes each channel
as a 64-bit x8 interface: its 8 `dqs` are the DIMM's lower-nibble strobes, its 8 `dm` are the
upper-nibble strobes, and the ECC byte is left out. With the x4 RDIMMs the board ships with, that
drives half the strobes as data masks. This design has both: the board file's x4 description for
the RDIMMs, and for x8 UDIMMs (this card's) LiteX's reading, which is right for them (section 3).

## 2. The accelerator on this board

**S = 2 slices, each on two DDR4 channels.** Each slice keeps the YPCB slice's memory system: the
two-channel adapter `otpu_native_dram` (unchanged; 512-bit beats, a 128-byte chunk is one beat on
each channel) in front of two channels. Each slice addresses 4 GiB (the ISA's 32-bit DRAM
addresses): 2 GiB of each of its channels' 16 GB (8 GB on this card's UDIMMs), so S = 2 uses 8 of
the 64 GB (32 GB) at a time. The
rest needs the ISA decision the spec defers (a per-slice window register, or far-memory DMA for
MoE expert pools on the card).

**Placement.** Slice 0 in SLR0 on C0 (local) and C1 (SLR1, one SLR crossing); slice 1 in SLR2 on C3
(local) and C2 (SLR1, one crossing); the shell (XDMA, control, the collective unit) in SLR1 with
the PCIe block. Pairing C1 with C2 (both SLR1) would leave the other slice on C0 and C3 at the die's
two ends, two crossings apart.

**Size.** The production YPCB image (e698dcd, MCOLS 4) is 176K LUT, 147K FF, 713 DSP and 607.5
BRAM36 tiles for one slice and its shell. One VU9P SLR has 394K LUTs, 788K FFs, 2,280 DSPs, 720
BRAM36 and 320 UltraRAMs. One slice per SLR fits with room to spare except in block RAM (84%):
the TMEM's six read copies (6 x 256 KB) belong in UltraRAM (TMEM_URAM=1: 48 URAM288 a slice in
the yosys estimate, 96 with a depth cascade), which takes the slice to 177.5 BRAM36 tiles there
(section 7). Two slices per SLR would not fit the LUTs. The
spare DSPs allow MCOLS = 8 per slice (+138 DSP per column on the DSP48E1, docs/mxu_systolic.md).
The yosys estimate for UltraScale+ is in section 7.

**Clocks.** The core clock comes from clk300_1 (SLR1), the same global buffer that feeds C1's
memory core, through the block design's MMCM (1200 MHz VCO, 1/8 steps). Development builds run at
100 MHz (the YPCB's rule: functional qualification first, Fmax afterwards). The DDR4 controllers
run at 150 MHz (DDR4-1200): USPDDRPHY's ISERDESE3s in component mode take 625 MHz at most on the
-2 part, so its sys4x clock (4 x the controller's) fails timing at DDR4-1600 and above (section
14). The DIMMs' own rate, DDR4-2400, would need the PHY's native mode.

## 3. Pins (`boards/bcu1525/constraints/`)

`scripts/gen_pins.py` writes `ddr4_c0..3.pins.xdc` and `bcu1525_pins.xdc` from AMD's VCU1525 board
file (XilinxBoardStore 2024.1, `boards/Xilinx/vcu1525/1.3/part0_pins.xml`, Apache-2.0) and checks
them against AMD's package file:

- every pin is in the package, in its channel's three banks and in its channel's SLR;
- each DQS pair k strobes the nibble of DQ 4k..4k+3: the four DQ pins share the strobe's
  byte-group nibble (e.g. 2L of bank 42), and the strobe pin is a DBC / QBC pin. This is MIG's x4
  order, and LiteDRAM's x4 PHY takes byte i on dqs[2i] and dqs[2i+1], so the order goes straight
  through.

Two pin sets per channel, one per DIMM kind (`DIMM`, section 6):

- `ddr4_c<d>.pins.xdc`, x4 RDIMMs (the board file's): DQ 0..71, all 18 strobe pairs, `par`, no DM.
- `ddr4_c<d>.udimm.pins.xdc`, x8 UDIMMs (this card's): DQ 0..63 and 8 strobe pairs. The board
  file's byte order is not the DIMM's: an RDIMM with ECC uses all nine bytes, so any order works
  there, but one of the board file's bytes 0..7 is the DIMM's check-bit byte CB0..7, which a UDIMM
  without ECC does not have, and its byte 8 (DQ 64..71) is a data byte. gen_pins.py's `CB_BYTE`
  (C0 byte 7, C1 byte 7, C2 byte 3, C3 byte 2) takes byte 8 in that byte's place. Those bytes'
  DRAMs never drove DQ in write leveling (0xff at all 512 DQS taps from the host, 2026-10-21; the
  BIOS's all-ones scan with the strobe on the other nibble's pair, 2026-10-26), and LiteX's
  `sqrl_xcu1525` platform (64 bits) leaves the same four out and takes byte 8: its eight bytes per
  channel, each byte's DQ, strobe and DM pins, are these files' (the order aside). Byte b's
  strobe, the DIMM's DQS_i, is the board's dqs[2b]; the other nibble's `_t` pin (the DIMM's
  DQS(9+i)_t) is that byte's DM_n / DBI_n, driven as `dm[i]` (POD12_DCI, 40 ohm output, no input
  settings). The check-bit byte's pins and the `dqs_c` of the strobes that are DM are left
  unconnected.

All checks pass. I/O standards: POD12_DCI for DQ / DQS, SSTL12_DCI for address and command,
DIFF_SSTL12_DCI for CK, LVCMOS12 for reset_n (MIG's DDR4 standards for UltraScale+), the drive
settings and the banks' INTERNAL_VREF 0.84 from LiteX's platform, LVDS + DQS_BIAS for the 300 MHz
clocks (board file 1.3). The board's LVCMOS12 outputs (LEDs, UART TX, I2C, the I2C mux reset) drive
8 mA: LVCMOS12's default, 12 mA, does not exist in the VU9P's HP banks (place_design's DRC BIVB-1).
The unused second rank's CS1_n..CS3_n are top ports (`cs_hi`) driven high
and CKE1 / ODT1 driven low; the RDIMM's parity input (`par`) is driven low. The register's parity
check is off after reset (unverified until the board runs).

Configuration properties: CONFIG_VOLTAGE 1.8, SPI x4, 85 MHz configuration rate, compression,
over-temperature shutdown (at the SYSMON's OT alarm, 84 C: section 14).

## 4. The DDR4 memory core (`boards/bcu1525/litedram/`, `litedram_udimm/`)

`tools/litedram/gen_core_ddr4.py` generates `otpu_ld4`, **one core per channel**, for one of two
DIMM kinds (`--dimm`): `rdimm` (the VCU1525's x4 ECC RDIMMs, in `litedram/`) or `udimm` (this
card's x8 UDIMMs, in `litedram_udimm/`; the default build's). The list below is the RDIMM core's;
the UDIMM core's differences follow it. The module is
generated once and instantiated four times. Each instance has its own MMCM from its channel's
300 MHz clock and its own IDELAYCTRL, is calibrated on its own and is placed by its banks. The
YPCB's core covers both of its channels in one module; here the channels are in three SLRs, each
with its own clock.

- LiteDRAM's `USPDDRPHY`, DDR4, `is_rdimm` (the RDIMM's register initialized by the init sequence),
  module MTA18ASF2G72PZ (16 banks in 4 groups, 128K rows, 1K columns: 16 GiB, a 28-bit beat
  address), 72 bits, x4 mode (18 strobes).
- Two native user ports through LiteDRAM's ECC (`tools/litedram/ecc_ports.py`: SECDED per 64-bit
  word, 512 user bits): the YPCB core's contract, so `otpu_mem_ch` and `otpu_native_dram` sit in
  front unchanged. `otpu_mem_ch` steers a beat to a port by beat address bit 7 (bank bit 0 under
  ROW_BANK_COLUMN with 8 KiB rows). The RDIMM's rows are 8 KiB too (1K columns x 8 bytes), and
  LiteDRAM's DDR4 bank address is {bg, ba}, so bit 7 is ba[0]: the even / odd split holds. Its beat
  address is 25 bits (2 GiB) against the core's 28 (16 GiB): the top supplies the upper three
  (0 for now, or a window register).
- Calibration: a VexRiscv (minimal) in the core runs LiteX's BIOS (lite console, a 64 KB ROM, 8 KB
  SRAM). At reset it runs `sdram_init()`: the RCD control words, the mode registers, write
  leveling, read leveling over the 512-tap IDELAYs and bitslips, write latency, a memtest of a
  1 GiB window. Its log is on `serial_tx` (115200 8N1): the top routes C1's core to the board's
  USB UART. The core's `ready` is `cal_ready` (set by the host) OR the DFII's hardware control
  (the BIOS hands the PHY to the controller after `sdram_init()`, whether its leveling and
  memtest passed or not). The YPCB's calibration (host `ddrcal.py` or its own firmware for
  WL7DDRPHY) does not apply to this PHY; `otpu-ddr4 calibrate` (section 11) calibrates a UDIMM
  core's DIMM again from the host, after the BIOS.
- CSRs on an AXI-Lite port in `ctl_clk` (BAR0 0x40000 + c x 0x10000), a BIST port, the ECC
  counters and `ded`. The port's 16-bit address is an offset in the CSR region, which the CPU's
  map puts at 0xF000_0000 (its BIOS ROM is at 0): the core adds the base. The first generated
  core (45dd9ddd) did not, so the host's CSR accesses reached the ROM; `opentpu.host.ddr4`'s map
  is csr.csv's addresses less that base (section 11).
- The XDC keeps LiteX's own timing constraints, by names inside the core: `create_project.tcl`
  scopes it to `otpu_ld4` (SCOPED_TO_REF).

The UDIMM core (identifier "openTPU LiteDRAM DDR4 UDIMM"):

- Module `GR2400D464L17S` (gen_core_ddr4.py): LiteDRAM's MT40A1G8 geometry (8 Gb x8: 16 banks in
  4 groups, 64K rows, 1K columns; 8 GiB, a 27-bit beat address) with JEDEC DDR4-2400R timings
  (tAA = tRCD = tRP = 14.16 ns, tRAS 32 ns, tFAW 25 ns). CL from tAA: 9 at DDR4-1200 (CWL 9,
  WR 10; the core is generated at 150 MHz, section 14), 12 at DDR4-1600.
  Rows are 8 KiB (1K columns x 8 bytes) and the bank address is {bg, ba}, so `otpu_mem_ch`'s port
  split by beat address bit 7 holds as on the RDIMM.
- 64 bits, x8 (8 strobes), DM pads (`with_dm`; LiteDRAM's DDR4 init enables the data mask in MR5,
  so the DM_n pins must be driven), no RCD words (`is_rdimm` off).
- No ECC: `ecc_ports.py`'s `NativePortsPlain` puts the same two 512-bit user ports straight on
  the controller, with the ECC block's CSRs (enable reads 0, the counters 0) so the host's map
  keeps its offsets; `ded` is 0. The BIST's beats are 512 bits, 8 byte lanes.
- The rest (BIOS, CSR port and base, XDC scoping, the clocking) is the RDIMM core's. Both cores
  come out of one generator run each from the same pinned LiteX; the RDIMM core regenerates byte
  for byte as committed.

Checked: the generator runs (LiteX at the YPCB core's pinned commits plus the VexRiscv and the
software packages listed in core.json), the BIOS compiles and fits, and yosys's `hierarchy -check`
of the module against its UltraScale+ cell library passes (every primitive and port exists).
Vivado 2026.1 synthesizes it out of context on the xcku5p (`make ipcheck`, 2026-10-11): 13.5K LUTs,
10.1K flip-flops, 19 block RAM tiles, 110 I/Os and one MMCM per core (four cores on the VU9P: about
54K LUTs and 76 tiles). LiteX's `USPIDELAYCTRL` sets the IDELAYCTRL's SIM_DEVICE to ULTRASCALE;
Vivado changes it to ULTRASCALE_PLUS at `link_design` (a critical warning), but not on the copies
it makes for an IODELAY_GROUP, and `write_bitstream`'s DRC refuses those (ADEF-911, the first VU9P
build, 2026-10-19). The generator sets ULTRASCALE_PLUS itself since.

Generating it needs the venv (`tools/litedram/check_core.sh --setup DIR`, plus the packages in
`core.json`) and a working `riscv64-unknown-elf-gcc`. On macOS the Homebrew one's `cc1` lacks
libisl; `RVSHIM` (`make core`) is a directory of `riscv64-unknown-elf-*` links to the working
`riscv64-elf-*`.

## 5. PCIe and the block design (`boards/bcu1525/vivado/bd.tcl`)

**Gen3 x16** (PCIE_LANES=16, the default; x8 is a knob). The host link is the YPCB's bottleneck
for MoE offload (Gen2 x8: about 3.0 GB/s placed; docs/offload.md section 6). Gen3 x16 is about 15.8
GB/s raw: 512-bit AXI at 250 MHz. The slot of the host this card goes into must be x16
electrically; Gen3 x8 (256-bit) is the fallback.

The block design keeps the YPCB's PCI identity (device 7028, class 1200, subsystem 10EE:4F54,
`xdma_rnum_rids` 8: docs/host.md "XDMA's H2C overrun") and its BAR0 layout (1 MB): control
registers at 0x0, the BMC's UART bridge at 0x20000 (section 13), the system monitor at 0x30000,
the four DDR4 cores' CSRs at 0x40000-0x7FFFF (0x10000, the YPCB core's R_MEMCAL, is unmapped). XDMA's DMA gets 8 GiB: card address bits
32:31 pick the channel j = 2s + c (slice s's channel c), 2 GiB each, the part of the DIMM the
slices address; the host's map is `opentpu.host.board.channel_map` (from CFG_SLICES and
CFG_DRAM; the YPCB's two channels at 0 and 2 GiB are the same rule). The rest of each DIMM waits
for the slices' window registers (0xA0 + 4s), and the DMA window grows with them. The die
temperature comes from the System Management Wizard (SYSMONE4). Its 10-bit code, given to the
board as the upper bits of the 12-bit TEMP code, needs UltraScale+'s transfer function (T = code12
x 509.314 / 4096 - 280.231, internal reference), not the XADC's: `regs.temp_c(code, family)`,
chosen in `Board.info` by CFG_PLATFORM's target.

## 6. Flow

`boards/bcu1525/`: `Makefile` (lint, pins, core, ipcheck, bit, impl, program, flash),
`run_vivado.sh` (the YPCB's knobs; S, PCIE_LANES; CORE_MHZ 100 by default; DIMM udimm / rdimm;
STEP full / synth / impl; SYNTH_THREADS, section 12), `vivado/create_project.tcl`
(xcvu9p-fsgd2104-2L-e; the shell's modules from `rtl/shell` once the multi-target layout lands,
`rtl/boards/ypcb-00338` before; the DSP48E2 cells from `rtl/tech/xilinxus`), `vivado/build.tcl`
(the YPCB's, plus per-SLR utilization, the SPI x4 flash image: 128 MB), `vivado/ip_check.tcl` (the
block design and the DDR4 core on any UltraScale+ part).

`make lint` (no Vivado) passes, for both DIMM kinds (Verilator twice, the second with
`-DOTPU_BCU_RDIMM`): Tcl syntax; the UDIMM build's 507 port bits on 507 distinct pins (the RDIMM
build's 587), each channel's XDC ports = the core's 17 pads (16 on the RDIMM core: no `dm`) + the 4
board-only ports; core.json's hashes.

`make ipcheck` (PART xcku5p-ffvb676-2-e: Vivado's free tier, a Kintex UltraScale+ with the PCIE4
block and GTY quads; IPCHECK_STEPS bd,bdsynth,ld4) passes (the build host, 2026-10-11, 9 min): `bd.tcl` builds and validates the block
design (XDMA Gen3 x16 at 512 bits with the PCI identity checked, the four 128-bit DMA masters
without IDs, the CSR windows, the System Management Wizard), its seven IPs synthesize out of
context, and the DDR4 core synthesizes (section 4). Its first run found two errors in `bd.tcl`,
fixed since: the System Management Wizard has no sequencer mode `Off` (`Default` now: the
sequencer's default channels, the die temperature among them), and the DMA ports' ID_WIDTH is
read-only on a block design's master port (the SmartConnect sets it; `bd.tcl` now checks that it
is 0, since the top has no ID signals and `otpu_mem_ch` answers in order). The run's other
critical warnings come from synthesizing the DDR4 core in the block design's project (its IPs'
XDC files find no module there).

`make synthcheck` (`scripts/synth_check.tcl`; PART as above) synthesizes the whole project on
another part: `create_project.tcl` with `OTPU_PART`, the real block design and DDR4 cores, the board
top at S = 2, development strategies, without the VU9P's pin constraints (their clocks kept). On the
xcku5p (the build host, 2026-10-11, 28 min) it passes with no error: the top fits the generated block
design wrapper, each slice maps to 716 DSP48E2 (1,432), and the late constraint files resolve on
the synthesized netlist: `bcu1525_top.tcl` finds each DIMM's clock domains (about 12.4K sys and
172 core_clk registers a core), and `bcu1525_floorplan.tcl` puts all its cells in their pblocks
(10, 9 and 19 of them) and marks 1,612 Laguna registers on each crossing channel and 4,238 and
1,938 on slice 0's and 1's control, status and collective (slice 0 also carries the activity
bundle). Its critical warnings: the IDELAYCTRLs' SIM_DEVICE (section 4, set by the generator
since) and the floorplan's SLR1
and SLR2 ranges, which a one-SLR part does not have. The utilization is not the VU9P's: the
xcku5p's 480 block RAM tiles are too few, so Vivado maps the rest of the memories to LUTs.

Programming: `openFPGALoader -b vcu1525` loads a bitstream over the FT4232H. The SPI flash needs a
spiOverJtag bitstream for xcvu9p-fsgd2104, which openFPGALoader does not ship (only the VCU118's
flga2104 package): build one in Vivado, or use Vivado's hardware manager (`program_hw_cfgmem`,
mt25qu01g-spi-x1_x2_x4).

stays. yosys packs each 8K x 32 bank into one URAM288 (two words per 72-bit row, the half chosen
by the address' low bit: the ~340 extra LUTs). Vivado may instead cascade two URAM288 per bank
in depth (96 a slice, 30%); either way the RTL is unchanged, so the simulations are bit-exact as
they are. Not run in Vivado (no UltraScale+ part).

The DSP count matches the production image's Vivado figure (713 with the DSP48E1 cells): each PE
and column end is one DSP48E2 here from the behavioural cells. The DSP48E2 cells themselves
(rtl/tech/xilinxus) match the behavioural ones in xsim with AMD's unisim models (Vivado
2026.1: the MXU cell testbench at seeds 3, 7 and 11, 3,984 trace lines each, identical), and
Vivado maps each to one DSP48E2: `make synthcheck` (section 6) gives 716 DSP48E2 a slice, the
YPCB's 716 DSP48E1. Block RAM is the limit,
as in section 2: the TMEM's read copies are 384 of the 561.5 tiles (an earlier version of this
section said 510, the RAMB36E2 count, which includes IMEM, the ACT RAM and the FIFOs). The two
slices fit the VU9P one per SLR as they are (78% of SLR0's and SLR2's block RAM, the memory
cores' few tiles on top); TMEM_URAM=1 (`run_vivado.sh`, `vivado/tmem_uram.xdc`) moves the TMEM
copies to UltraRAM and brings a slice to 177.5 tiles (24.7%), leaving room for MCOLS = 8 or a
third slice in SLR1 by the block RAM, LUT and DSP counts. The LUT figure is logic only before placement (Vivado
packs LUT pairs: the production image is 176K LUTs with its shell, a 2-channel LiteDRAM and
XDMA).

## 10. SLR floorplan (the LiteDRAM image)

`constraints/bcu1525_floorplan.tcl` (unmanaged Tcl, read late in implementation) puts slice 0
(its `otpu_slice`, `otpu_native_dram`, DIMM C0's core and bridge) in SLR0, slice 1 (the same with
C3) in SLR2 and the shell in SLR1: the block design (XDMA sits on GTY quads 224-227 in SLR1), the
control block, the collective unit, the trace and DIMMs C1 and C2's cores and bridges. Each slice
has one channel in its own SLR and one in SLR1 (board channels 1 and 3), so each of the two outer
SLR boundaries carries one slice's second native port (~1,150 signals in core_clk) plus the
slice's control, status and collective traffic (~1,650 more signals, 1,110 down and 543 up,
plus slice 0's activity bundle). Why this split:

- Block RAM decides it: a slice's TMEM copies take 78% of an SLR's block RAM (section 7), so one
  slice per outer SLR, and SLR1, which has the PCIe block anyway, keeps what is small.
- The crossings stay in core_clk (100 MHz in development builds): each `otpu_mem_ch` sits with
  its DIMM's core, so the 200 MHz controller-side buses never leave their SLR; the slow side of
  the bridge crosses instead, through two `otpu_native_reg` (`otpu_bcu_sys` SLL, u_tx with the
  slice, u_rx in SLR1). Every crossing signal leaves and enters a flip-flop; the flop-to-flop
  ones (read data, the write count, the error flag, the skid buffers' output registers) carry
  `USER_SLL_REG` so the placer puts them in the Laguna sites. The register slices keep the
  native port's contract (order, no read backpressure; the write count a cycle late, so WR_IDLE
  too): `test_tiny_qwen3_two_slices_on_board_model[10]` runs the board model with them on
  channels 1 and 3, bit-exact against the ISA simulator.
- Everything else between a slice and the shell is registered too (`otpu_board` at S > 1, the
  `g_xs` generate; S = 1 keeps the old wiring, which `tools/synth/board_equiv.sh` proves equal):
  two `otpu_xr` stages each way per slice, `u_*_tx` on the sending side and `u_*_rx` on the
  receiving one, `USER_SLL_REG` on both. Down: the core and system resets, LOAD (start, address,
  count) and the arguments, for every slice (slice 0 too, so the slices still start in the same
  cycle). Up: the status (LD_BUSY, HALTED, ERROR, WAIT_TO, the idle flags, ICOUNT, instructions
  and DRAM beats this cycle) and slice 0's activity bundle (the counters, the trace). They reach
  the control block XR = 2 cycles late, so the LOAD window that covers the loader's busy
  (`otpu_ctrl` LDP) is 2 + 2 XR at S > 1.
- The collective: at S = 1 its TMEM grant is the AND of the slices' grants in the same cycle,
  which at S = 2 is a combinational path through all three SLRs. At S > 1 `otpu_coll` runs
  unchanged against `otpu_coll_hub` (SLR1), whose grant comes from its own registers, and each
  slice gets an `otpu_coll_port` in its own SLR that presents the collective's reads (one in
  flight) and writes (a FIFO of 8, returned as credits) on the slice's collective TMEM port and
  has its own arbiter grant them. The unit's ack reaches the slices once every port has written
  everything. The ISA's rule that a GATHER's source and destination do not overlap makes the order
  of a slice's reads and writes free. The price: a read waits for the round trip (about 7 cycles),
  so a GATHER moves one beat per round trip instead of one per cycle. Checked: the board model at
  S = 2 (`test_tiny_qwen3_two_slices_on_board_model`, both channel settings, with the crossings)
  is bit-exact against the ISA simulator.
- The collective's reads in flight (`otpu_board` COLL_PIPE 1, the two-slice WG targets'; absent: 0, the
  hub above): the hub grants the unit on read credits, NR of them (16 at XRS 2-4), so the unit
  issues a read every cycle; its writes (one a read, in order) are taken as each read's lanes and
  addresses, and the hub sends each read's write itself with the read's data once that is back
  (the reads' slices in issue order, each slice's data in a FIFO of its own, the oldest read's
  write from flip-flops when every port has room). Each port queues its reads (a FIFO of NR) and
  asks the oldest from flip-flops, a read a granted cycle; its write FIFO is F deep (16) to cover
  the credit loop. Each slice sees the same reads and writes in the same order with the same
  data; only the timing changes. That relies on the ISA's rule that a GATHER's source and
  destination are apart, which the simulation checks in the hub at either setting (a read against
  writes still in flight to that slice, a write against reads still out; `+coll_overlap_ok` turns
  it off). XRS (default 2) is the crossings' stages each way, with the stages between as an
  `otpu_xr` chain; NR and F follow from it (the read loop is 2 XRS + 6 cycles, the write credit
  loop 2 XRS + 4). `tests/test_coll_hub.py` (`sim/verilator/tb_coll_hub.sv`) drives the hub and
  ports alone: random GATHERs and BARs at S 2-4 with grants withheld, resets mid-GATHER, the
  overlap check against an illegal GATHER, and a GATHER's cycles by size.

`constraints/bcu1525_top.tcl` carries the top's clock-domain crossings (each DIMM's CSR port, its
calibration and ECC flags, its reset into the CSR clock, the control masters' reset, the CDC
waivers for XDMA's read data), the YPCB's `otpu_top_native.tcl` per DIMM; `create_project.tcl`
adds it, the YPCB's scoped `otpu_mem_ch.tcl` (one per bridge) and the floorplan. The cell and
pin names both files use are checked against Vivado's own elaboration of the top
(`scripts/elab_check.tcl`: `synth_design -rtl` on an installed part with the lint stand-ins) and
applied to the synthesized netlist on the xcku5p (`make synthcheck`, section 6: every pblock cell
and crossing register found); the SLR placement and timing need the VU9P.

## 11. DDR4 bring-up (`opentpu/host/ddr4.py`, `otpu-ddr4`)

Each DIMM's core calibrates itself (its BIOS, section 4) and logs on the UART; the host reaches
each core's CSRs in BAR0 at `R_DDR4` + d x `DDR4_STRIDE` (0x40000 + d x 0x10000; `XdmaTransport`
maps BAR0 up to 0x80000 now, the first 0x40000 if the driver maps less). Board channel j = 2s + c
is on DIMM `CHANNEL_DIMM[j]` = C0, C1, C3, C2 (the top's `dimm()`; tests/test_ddr4.py checks the
two agree, and bcu1525_top.tcl's list). The tool runs only on a card whose bitstream (register
map 4) names a target with LiteDRAM DDR4 cores (CFG_PLATFORM).

- `otpu-ddr4` (status): per DIMM, the core's identifier ("openTPU LiteDRAM DDR4", or "... DDR4
  UDIMM": the CSR path works and the window is a core's; the identifier picks the core's CSR map,
  `litedram4/` or `litedram4u/`, `data_of`), the scratch register written and read back, calibrated
  (`calibrated (host)`: `cal_ready`; `calibrated (BIOS, unchecked)`: DFII hardware control only,
  which the BIOS sets whether or not its leveling and memtest passed), the ECC on and its counters;
  STATUS CALIB0 (C0 and C3) / CALIB1 (C1 and C2) and ECC_DED. Exit 1 when a DIMM is not
  calibrated or its scratch register fails.
- `otpu-ddr4 bist [--dimm D] [--gib G] [--passes N]`: the core's BIST (ld_test.py's: 576-bit raw
  beats past the ECC, random then address data, written then read and checked; wrong beats per
  byte lane and the wrong bits) over the DIMM's first G GiB, with the GB/s each way (one 64-byte
  beat a 150 MHz cycle is 9.6 GB/s: the UDIMM core's 8 GiB read 8.54 GB/s on the card). The BIST leaves the check bits wrong, so it clears
  SCRATCH's DRAM_INIT: the next `BoardBackend` scrubs the DRAM. The full 16 GiB of a DIMM is
  2^28 beats, within the BIST's length register. A UDIMM core's BIST: 512-bit beats, 8 lanes, 8 GiB
  a DIMM.
- `otpu-ddr4 ecc [--clear]`: the counters (64-bit words corrected / uncorrectable since the last
  clear). `Board.scrub` clears them after its zeros, as it does the YPCB's (`memcal.ecc`), so
  STATUS ECC_DED counts from there.
- `otpu-ddr4 calibrate [--dimm D] [--vref PCT] [--gib G]` (a UDIMM core's DIMMs): the host's
  calibration after the BIOS's (`calibrate_dimm`), then the BIST over G GiB. Write leveling per
  lane: the DRAM's feedback of CK at its DQS (MR1 A7, `wlevel_en` / `wlevel_strobe`) over the DQS
  delay's counts 0 .. 511 - tCK / 4 (the PHY adds tCK / 4 to the count), the DQS at the scan's
  last 0 -> 1 edge (the earlier one when no write bitslip reaches the last), the DQ delays with
  it; then ddrcal's write latency (the write bitslip per lane, every lane per scan) and read
  leveling (the read bitslip and IDELAY tap at the centre of the widest window). The BIOS takes
  its scan's longest 1-window instead, which may start before the scan: the DQS a tCK early, a
  write bitslip of 8 that the PHY does not have (DIMM C1's lane 6, 2026-10-21). EN_VTC is off
  while the delays move and on after; the DQS delays' counters have no reset (`wdly_dqs_rst`
  leaves them), so the host increments round to a count. `--vref` sets VrefDQ through MR6's
  training mode (range 1: 60 + 0.65 x value % of VDDQ; the init leaves 60 %). A DIMM passes when
  every lane has a write latency and a read window, the check at the chosen taps is clean and
  so is the BIST: `cal_ready` 1 (`calibrated (host)`). Otherwise the DIMM is left in software
  control (`NOT calibrated`: the core's ready 0, STATUS CALIB drops). It writes over the DRAM, so
  it clears DRAM_INIT as `bist` does. Not the RDIMM core's DIMMs (their write leveling goes
  through the RCD).

On arrival: program the bitstream, read the BIOS's log on the UART (C1's core), `otpu-ddr4`,
`otpu-ddr4 bist --gib 8` (each UDIMM whole; 16 for the RDIMMs), `otpu-ddr4 ecc`, then `otpu-selftest`. Checked on a
fake card (tests/test_ddr4.py: the windows, the identifier, scratch, calibration, ECC clear, a
BIST with wrong beats in one lane) and against the generated core's CSR map and its address
decode; not on a board.

Fixed with it in the core generator: the CSR port's base (section 4) and `sdram_init.py`'s `phy`
settings, written as JSON (`"rdimm": true`, which Python's exec, how the host reads the file,
rejected) and now as a Python literal. Regenerated: only those two lines of `otpu_ld4.v` changed
(and its comment header's order), the BIOS ROM, the XDC and the ports are the same.

## 12. Day one: license to first tokens

The commands in order, on the build host and the card's host, and what to look at
after each. Stop at the first step that does not look as described; each has its log or report.

**1. License and device files (build host).** Install the Virtex UltraScale+ devices into
Vivado (the installer, or Help > Add Design Tools or Devices), add the license (Vivado License
Manager, `vlm`, or `XILINXD_LICENSE_FILE`), then:

    vivado -mode batch -nojournal -nolog -source boards/bcu1525/scripts/preflight.tcl \
        -tclargs xcvu9p-fsgd2104-2L-e

Look for `PREFLIGHT ok: xcvu9p-fsgd2104-2L-e is installed and licensed`. Exit 2 means the part
is missing (the message lists the families that are installed); exit 3 means the license: the
device files are installed but Vivado hides the part (it lists only the parts a license enables),
or synthesis or `opt_design` on the part was refused. `run_vivado.sh` runs the same check first.

**2. Offline checks and Vivado's elaboration on the real part.**

    make -C boards/bcu1525 lint
    vivado -mode batch -nojournal -source boards/bcu1525/scripts/elab_check.tcl \
        -tclargs xcvu9p-fsgd2104-2L-e

`lint`: `offline checks: ok` and no Verilator warnings. `elab_check`: `ELAB-CHECK done: 0
missing` and `ELAB-CHECK TMEM bank memories: 96`. Until now it ran only on xc7k480t.

**3. The first bitstream: 100 MHz, default strategies, TMEM in block RAM.**

    FAST=1 CORE_MHZ=100 boards/bcu1525/run_vivado.sh        # build/vivado_bcu1525/

`DIMM=udimm` (the default) builds for this card's x8 UDIMMs, `DIMM=rdimm` for the VCU1525's x4
RDIMMs (section 4). On a 32 GB host run it in two steps, so that implementation starts
in a fresh Vivado (the synthesis's parent keeps ~6 GB after `open_run`), with synthesis's helper
processes capped (by default it forks `general.maxThreads` - 1 of them, ~19 GB together) and
three IP runs at a time:

    STEP=synth SYNTH_THREADS=2 JOBS=3 FAST=1 CORE_MHZ=100 boards/bcu1525/run_vivado.sh
    STEP=impl JOBS=3 FAST=1 CORE_MHZ=100 boards/bcu1525/run_vivado.sh

Implementation peaked at 16.9 GB (phys_opt, 2026-10-19).

In `create_project.log` / `build.log`:
- The IPs generate. The XDMA settings check in `bd.tcl` (pf0_device_id 7028, class 120000,
  subsystem 4F54) prints no mismatch.
- `bcu1525_floorplan.tcl: pb_slice0 in SLR0, N of N cells`, and the same for pb_slice1 and
  pb_shell, all N of N. The Laguna lines are `channel 1/3, ... Laguna registers` and `slice 0/1's
  control / status / collective, ... Laguna registers`.
- `bcu1525_top.tcl: channel j on DIMM Cd, ...` four times, and `DIMM Cd IODELAY_GROUP otpu_ldd: 1
  IDELAYCTRL, 189 delays` four times (each core's own IDELAYCTRL, grouped with its delays: four
  ungrouped IDELAYCTRLs fail placement's DRC PLIDC-7).
- `create_project.log` has no `the VU9P's pin constraints left out` line (that is for another
  part only). The first VU9P build (2026-10-19) left them out: `bd.tcl`, sourced into
  `create_project.tcl`, set the same variable (`VU9P`) to 1, so the part compared as another one.
  Implementation then placed every port itself; `write_bitstream`'s DRC stopped it (NSTD-1,
  UCIO-1: no I/O standard, no LOC), as it does any build without the pins.
- A critical warning to expect: AVAL-46 on each core's MMCM (FVCO 1600.16 MHz: Vivado computes it
  from the 3.333 ns period; 300 MHz x 16 / 3 is 1600 exactly, the limit).
- No other `CRITICAL WARNING` from the two constraint files.
- `bitstream: .../otpu.bit` and no `WARNING: timing not met`.

In `reports/`:
- `util_slr.rpt`: block RAM about 80% in SLR0 and SLR2, SLR1 well below.
- `util.rpt`: about 1,434 DSP48E2 (717 a slice; section 7).
- `timing_summary.rpt`: WNS >= 0 at 100 MHz. The worst paths should not be SLR crossings;
  `report_timing -through [get_cells -hier -filter USER_SLL_REG]` shows them.
- `cdc.rpt`: only the waived XDMA read-data crossings.
- `drc.rpt`: no errors.

A build that fails timing is still loadable for bring-up at a lower clock (CORE_MHZ=80).

**4. Load it (JTAG; the card's host).** The board's micro-USB is JTAG and the UART. From a
Vivado on the card's host (hardware manager):

    open_hw_manager; connect_hw_server; open_hw_target
    set d [lindex [get_hw_devices xcvu9p*] 0]; current_hw_device $d
    set_property PROGRAM.FILE otpu.bit $d; program_hw_devices $d

Then `otpu-setup --rescan`, and `otpu-setup --check`, which should report:
- the card `[10ee:7028] subsystem 10ee:4f54`, class 120000;
- `link 8.0 GT/s PCIe x16 (the card's own speed and width)`;
- bound to xdma, `/dev/xdma0_*` present, the ID register `0x4F545055` ("OTPU").

A link at x8 or Gen1 means the slot or a lane problem (the check says so); `0xffffffff` means
the card was reprogrammed after enumeration (rescan).

**5. The DDR4 cores' own log (the UART, 115200 8N1).** Open the board's USB UART (`ls
/dev/ttyUSB*`; `screen /dev/ttyUSBn 115200`) before loading the bitstream: C1's core prints
LiteX's BIOS banner, then
`Initializing SDRAM @0x40000000...`, the write leveling and read leveling tables per module,
and `Memtest OK` for its 1 GiB window. A module with no window in the read leveling, or `Memtest
KO`, is that DIMM's problem. Only C1 is on the UART; `otpu-ddr4` covers all four.

**6. The memory over the CSRs.**

    otpu-ddr4                              # every DIMM: identifier, scratch, calibrated, ECC
    otpu-ddr4 calibrate                    # each UDIMM again from the host (section 11)
    otpu-ddr4 bist --gib 8                 # each DIMM whole (16 on RDIMMs): write + read-check
    otpu-ddr4 ecc

`status`:
- every DIMM `'openTPU LiteDRAM DDR4 UDIMM'`, `scratch ok (0x12345678)`, `calibrated (BIOS,
  unchecked)` before `calibrate` and `calibrated (host)` after it, ECC
  `off: 0 corrected, 0 uncorrectable` (an RDIMM build: `'openTPU LiteDRAM DDR4'`, ECC `on`);
- `STATUS ...: CALIB0 1 CALIB1 1`.

A wrong identifier or `scratch FAILED` is the CSR path: the window map in `bd.tcl`, or the core
held in reset (an access to a core in reset answers SLVERR).

`bist`:
- `errors 0` on every DIMM for both data kinds. Errors confined to one byte lane are that lane's
  DQ/DQS (the per-lane counts and the wrong bits say which);
- about 9.6 GB/s peak each way at DDR4-1200 (one 64-byte beat per 150 MHz cycle; the BIST
  writes 8.44 and reads 8.54 GB/s).

`ecc`: no uncorrectable words. The BIST clears the scrub marker, so the next BoardBackend
scrubs (writes zeros over the DRAM once).

**7. The accelerator.**

    otpu-smi
    otpu-selftest
    otpu-selftest --model qwen3

`otpu-smi`'s config line: `bcu1525 (S=2, 2 channels x 2048 MiB), core 100 MHz, DDR4-1200`.

`otpu-selftest` passes link, config, calib, scrub, regs, then addr (`channel 0..3 ok`: every
slice's channels), pattern, bandwidth (H2C / C2H GB/s at Gen3 x16), kernel, vops, stream and ecc.

`--model qwen3`: greedy decoding equal to the ISA simulator, token for token. That runs on
slice 0; the two-slice programs (`board_config(S=2)`) next.

**8. Record it.** The build's sha (BUILD_ID), the reports above, the `otpu-ddr4` and
`otpu-selftest` output, the BIOS log; then the open items of section 9 (DDR4-2400, the clock).

## 13. The board management controller: VCCINT's power (`otpu_bmc_uart`, `opentpu/host/bmc.py`)

The card's power is not on a bus the FPGA drives. Its board management controller (BMC, the
VCU1525's MSP432 "satellite controller") reads the regulators on its own I2C bus, and the FPGA
reaches the BMC only through a UART: BB19 is the BMC's RX, BA19 its TX (LVCMOS12; the VCU1525
master XDC). The line (115200 baud 8E1) and the BMC's protocol come from the card vendor's
management tools: recovered from them, not published by the vendor. A small helper image holding
only the UART read the VCCINT regulator that way on the card (2026-10-21; its bitstream and
scripts are kept in a separate repository, BCU1525-CardManagement). This section is that path
inside the openTPU image, so the power can be read while the accelerator runs.

**The bridge.** `rtl/boards/bcu1525/otpu_bmc_uart.sv`: an AXI4-Lite slave in core_clk at BAR0
0x20000 (`bd.tcl`'s M_AXI_BMC, a seventh SmartConnect master), two 256-byte FIFOs and the UART.
It knows no BMC command: the host frames the requests and checks the replies. CAPS2 bit4 (register
map 5; otpu_ctrl `HAS_BMC`, set by `otpu_bcu_sys`) announces it. Its registers keep AMD's UART
Lite layout (PG142) at 0x0-0xC:

| Offset | Name | Access | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0x00 | RX | RO | bit31 VALID and [7:0] the oldest byte, taken from the FIFO by the read; 0 when empty |
| 0x04 | TX | W | [7:0] into the TX FIFO; dropped when it is full (STATUS bit8) |
| 0x08 | STATUS | RO | bit0 RX valid, bit1 RX full, bit2 TX empty, bit3 TX full, bit5 overrun, bit6 framing error, bit7 parity error, bit8 a TX byte dropped, bit9 TX idle (the FIFO empty, the last stop bit sent), bit10 the RX line; bits 5-8 cleared by the read |
| 0x0C | CTRL | W | bit0 empties the TX FIFO (a byte on the line finishes), bit1 the RX FIFO |
| 0x10 | LEVEL | RO | [15:0] bytes in the RX FIFO, [31:16] in the TX FIFO |
| 0x14 | ID | RO | 0x424D4332 ("BMC2") with the watch (below), 0x424D4331 ("BMC1") without |
| 0x18 | DIV | RO | core clock cycles per bit |
| 0x1C | HOLD | RW | bit0 the host asks for the line (1 asks, 0 gives it back; cleared when the watch takes it back), bit1 HELD |
| 0x20 | W_HOT | RO | [15:0] the hottest of the three temperatures below (signed, quarter degrees C), [31:16] the oldest of their ages: what the governor sees |
| 0x24-0x34 | W_P0, W_P1, W_CTL, W_PIN, W_VIN | RO | [15:0] the LINEAR11 word of page 0's and page 1's READ_TEMPERATURE_1, READ_TEMPERATURE_2 (the controller's die), READ_PIN and READ_VIN; [31:16] ms since it was read (saturating, 0xFFFF before the first) |
| 0x38 | W_FW | RO | the BMC firmware's version (0 until it answered) |
| 0x3C | W_CNT | RO | [15:0] replies taken, [31:16] exchanges failed |

STATUS adds bit11 HELD (always set without the watch), bit12 REVOKED (sticky: the watch took the
line back) and bit13 the watch stopped (a firmware the protocol is not known for).

The receiver samples each bit at its middle, drops a start bit shorter than half a bit, and after
a framing error waits for the line to go high. `sim/verilator/tb_bmc_uart.sv` checks the
registers, both FIFOs through a loopback, every frame on the line, the error flags, the FIFOs'
limits and resets (`tests/test_bmc.py`). Cost (Vivado, out of context on the VU9P): 315 LUTs,
80 of them the FIFOs' LUT RAM, 171 flip-flops, no block RAM; 2.4 ns of slack at 250 MHz after
synthesis.

**The protocol** (`opentpu/host/bmc.py`'s docstring has the bytes): frames `5C 02 ... 5C 03` with
`5C` doubled inside; inside, a command, its payload and a 16-bit sum. 0x0C asks the BMC's
firmware version (0x10002..0x10005 are the versions the vendor's tools accept; the host sends no
I2C request to another); 0x0A is an I2C write-then-read on the BMC's bus. The regulator answers
with the SMBus PEC, checked on every read. Each session (under an flock,
`<run dir>/<device>.bmc.lock`) starts by emptying both FIFOs and sending `00 5C 03`, which ends a
frame left half sent at the BMC; each request starts with the RX FIFO emptied. A wrong PEC, a
lost reply or a line error is retried twice after a new sync.

**The watch** (`rtl/boards/bcu1525/otpu_bmc_watch.sv`, in the bridge since 2026-10-27, ID "BMC2").
The host's guard read the regulator's temperatures in sessions, so the regulator's limit held only
while the host did. The watch reads them in the fabric, for the governor (section 14). Every
500 ms a round: the sync and 5 ms of quiet, the firmware's version until the BMC has answered it
(not one of 0x10002..0x10005: the watch stops for good and STATUS bit13 says so), then five
requests to the LTC3884, the bytes `bmc.py` sends: page 0's and page 1's READ_TEMPERATURE_1
(PAGE_PLUS_READ), READ_TEMPERATURE_2, READ_PIN and READ_VIN. They are constants in the RTL (read
only, so nothing in the fabric can change a setting either). A reply counts when its frame, length,
command, sum, the BMC's I2C status, the block count and the PEC check, and for a temperature when it
is between -40 and 150 C; its word replaces the reading and the reading's age restarts. A reply
that fails or does not come within 200 ms counts in W_CNT's failures, and 200 ms of quiet and the
sync come before the next request. A temperature that stops being read ages, and the governor
takes an old one as a hot one.

The line is the watch's between the host's holds. A session (`bmc.Bmc`) asks (HOLD), waits for HELD
(the watch grants it at the end of a round; the session waits up to 3 s), syncs, makes its requests
and gives the line back at its end. 1000 ms after the grant the watch takes the line back by itself
(STATUS REVOKED), so a killed session cannot keep the regulator unread; the session's request then
fails and it holds again. Without the line the host's TX bytes wait in their FIFO and its RX FIFO
takes nothing. `bmc.watch(t)` reads W_HOT .. W_CNT, no request sent, safe beside another process's
session; `opentpu.host.gov.Guard` uses it on a BMC2 bridge (a few register reads instead of a
session that took about 3 s over JTAG) and a session on a BMC1 one.

`sim/verilator/tb_bmc_watch.sv` scripts a BMC on the line: the rounds' bytes, good replies, each
check failing in turn, a temperature out of range, a 5C escape in a reply, the host's hold in idle
and in mid-round, a revoke, and a firmware that stops the watch (`tests/test_bmc.py`:
`test_bmc_watch_rtl`, `test_watch_lends_the_line`, `test_watch_readings`). Each mutation of the RTL
tried fails the testbench (one that did not, the host's RX FIFO taking bytes on the watch's line,
added its LEVEL checks). Cost (yosys `synth_xilinx -family xcup`, the bridge with and without it):
about 920 LUTs and 540 flip-flops more, under 0.1 % of the VU9P.

**Read only.** The bytes the host writes to the regulator are one command code from the read
tables (`bmc.READS`: telemetry, status, identification, settings, limits, peaks) or
PAGE_PLUS_READ (0x06) of one, which reads a page's register without changing PAGE. The bridge
refuses anything else before it reaches the UART (`Bmc.exchange`; the tests check that no
send-byte command, PAGE, WRITE_PROTECT, MFR_RESET, MFR_CLEAR_PEAKS, EEPROM or fault-log command is
in the tables). Nothing sets a voltage, a limit or OPERATION.

**What it measures.** The regulator at 0x5B is an LTC3884 (MFR_SPECIAL_ID 0x4Cxx), VCCINT's.
READ_PIN is its input power on the 12 V side (READ_VIN x READ_IIN through its input sense
resistor), the regulator's loss included. It is one value for both pages; never add page reads
of it. READ_POUT, READ_IOUT, READ_VOUT and READ_TEMPERATURE_1 are per page (output channel), and
READ_TEMPERATURE_2 is the controller's die. None of it is the card's power: the DIMMs, the
transceivers, VCCAUX and the I/O banks are on other regulators. Nor is all of VCCINT certain:
UG1268 v1.0 (the VCU1525's, 2017) puts two LTC3874 slave controllers (U39, U40) beside it, off
PMBus, and estimates VCCINT's current as the LTC3884's two phases times three. The pages'
READ_IOUT and READ_POUT may then see a third of it, and READ_PIN only what passes the LTC3884's
input sense resistor. `otpu-smi` reports READ_PIN; take every figure as a lower bound until one
is compared with a meter at the wall (the card's load minus the factory image's idle).

**The SYSMON's registers, read only when they must be.** An AXI read of any of the wizard's DRP
registers (0x30400 and up) can stop its temperature bus, the governor's die, for good: about
one read in 1000 lands in the cycle that makes the wizard's arbiter drop the bus's own read
([bcu1525_sysmon.md](bcu1525_sysmon.md); one image's froze at 46.05 C after `wg_bringup up`
read five of them). So `otpu-smi` reads none by default; the alarm and OT
come from the governor's STATUS (the SYSMON's ALM0 and OT lines). The governor's die is gone when
it is not valid, or when it has read the same for 60 s over an `-l` run (gov.FROZEN_S, fan_pid's
`--stale-s`: a live die moves in 0.5 C codes); then a note says "gov frozen" and each sample reads
the SYSMON's temperature register alone (one read, which cannot make a stopped bus worse) as the
die. `--sysmon` reads all 13 registers every sample (the die, its maximum and minimum, the
supplies, the alarm thresholds), and notes a governor die 5 C or more off the register's.

A note follows for a standing SYSMON alarm (the temperature alarm, OT), for what refuses GO in

governor's die gone, and for a stopped watch. On a BMC2 bridge `otpu-smi` sends the BMC nothing,

## 14. A card without a fan: the thermal governor, image M and image F

On 2026-10-21 the passive card, in a desktop case with no airflow, ran the first openTPU image's
VU9P to 112 C (section 13). The rule since: the die and the card's regulators stay under 85 C.
Three layers keep it, the inner ones in the bitstream so that they hold with the host gone:

1. **The SYSMON** (both block designs, Continuous sequencer): its over-temperature alarm at 84 C
   (reset 65 C) with the bitstream's OVERTEMPSHUTDOWN unconfigures the FPGA about 10 ms later
   (UG580); its temperature alarm ALM[0], up at 80 C and down at 70 C, goes to the governor.
2. **The governor** (`rtl/boards/bcu1525/otpu_bcu_gov.sv`, BAR0 0x10000; its header has the
   registers): SAFE after configuration, every power domain off. The host asks GO; the governor
   grants RUN only with the die at or below RESUME, no SYSMON alarm, a valid reading and a
   heartbeat (KICK) within HB_MS. In RUN it drops to SAFE by itself on the die at TRIP, either
   SYSMON alarm, a lost reading or no heartbeat for HB_MS. TRIP and RESUME clamp at the build's
   limits (79.9 C and 69.9 C: a host cannot set them higher); STATUS keeps why RUN ended.
   With the BMC's watch (section 13; HAS_REG, both images since 2026-10-27) the VCCINT
   regulator too: GO needs the hottest of its controller's die and its two pages at or below
   REG_RESUME (75.0 C) and read within REG_STALE (10 s); RUN ends with it at REG_TRIP (78.0 C)
   or unread for REG_STALE (STATUS causes bit21 and bit22). REG_TRIP and REG_RESUME clamp at the
   build's 78.0 and 75.0 C, REG_STALE within 1 to 30 s; registers 0x30-0x44 (REG, REG_TRIP,
   REG_RESUME, REG_STALE, REG_AGE, REG_LIMITS), STATUS bit13 REG_OK, bit14 REG_COOL and bit15
   REG_WARN (2 C below REG_TRIP, on led[2]). The regulator's limit then holds with the host
   gone, as the die's does. A die's code that has not changed for TEMP_STALE_MS (60 s) is no
   reading (on images whose governor has TEMP_STALE_MS): TEMP_VALID drops, RUN ends (STATUS cause bit23,
   thermal) and GO waits until it moves. The SYSMON wizard's temperature bus, the governor's
   die, can stop for good after an AXI read of the wizard's DRP registers
   ([bcu1525_sysmon.md](bcu1525_sysmon.md); on the card: 46.05 C for 3.5 h while
   the die rose to 80 C), and a live die's code moves within seconds. The age counts while the
   temperature alarm is clear and holds while it stands; TEMP_AGE (0x48: [16:0] ms, bit31
   stale) and TEMP_STALE (0x4C: the build's limit, 0 on older builds) show it, and otpu-smi and
   fan_pid take the governor's verdict from them.
3. **The host's guard** (`opentpu.host.gov.Guard`): kicks every 0.5 s while the die's reading
   (every 2 s) is fresh, reads the VCCINT regulator's temperatures through the BMC (section 13)
   every 5 s in its own thread, and goes SAFE itself at a die of 81 C, a regulator at 78 C or
   the regulator unread for 60 s (on a BMC2 bridge it reads the watch's registers and takes
   their age); it reconfigures the FPGA from the flash (JPROGRAM: the factory
   image) at 83 / 82 C or when the governor has not answered six kicks in a row. Its log is a
   CSV (`thermal*.csv`).

**What SAFE turns off.** The accelerator: held in reset and, in image F, its clock stopped
(`acc_clk`, below). Each DIMM's LiteDRAM core: in reset, its MMCM with it, so its PHY, its
controller and the DIMM's clock stop (no refresh: the DRAM's contents are lost, and the core
calibrates again when it comes back). In front of each switched block a gate answers for it while
it is off: `otpu_axil_off` on the DIMMs' CSRs and the control registers, `otpu_axi_off` on each
channel's DMA path (it finishes the bursts in flight, then answers every burst itself). What runs
in SAFE: XDMA, the interconnect, the SYSMON, the governor, the BMC's UART and the JTAG masters,
and the die's static power.

**The card's idle.** With every DIMM off the die sat at 53-55 C at 16:50 on 2026-10-26 and crept
to 63 C by 18:20 as the case warmed (the VCCINT regulator's input power 1.3-1.8 W the whole time,
its controller 60-70 C). One DIMM calibrating and under the BIST added 2-3 C. By 20:00, after
three hours of tests, the idle die averaged 66 C (its readings 64.5-67.5 C) and the regulator's
controller 72-73 C, the host itself idle (its CPU at 38 C): the card's own idle power, with no air
moving, heats it that far. RESUME was clamped at 65.0 C in the first builds (image M be9d1d1b,
image F b283ab76); with the idle die above it, GO came only on a reading's dip (C2 waited 5
minutes at 19:46, C3 more than 20 from 19:56) and then not at all. The clamp is 69.9 C from
image F's second build (RESUME_MAX 704: TRIP_MAX less the governor's two WARN margins, 10 C), and
the SYSMON's alarm clears at 70 C (it cleared at 55 C: an alarm on a card idling above 55 C would
have refused GO until the FPGA was configured again). RESUME only decides when RUN may start:
TRIP (79.9 C), the alarm (80 C), the guard (81 / 78 C), JPROGRAM (83 / 82 C) and OT (84 C) are
unchanged. A fan on the card's heatsink is what brings the idle down. With it, fan_pid holds the
idle die at 60 C (section 15), above the governor's RESUME after configuration (RESUME_DEF, 55 C),
so `scripts/wg_bringup.py up` sets RESUME first: `--resume` (65 C), at most RESUME_MAX and TRIP
less 20 codes (10 C), written again with TRIP after its thermal trip and recorded in wg.json. On
2026-10-29 at 11:47, without it, GO never came (go_cool retries for 30 min; the user stopped it).
The register goes back to 55 C with every configuration.

**Image M** (`IMAGE=mem`: `vivado/create_project_mem.tcl`, `bd_mem.tcl`,
`otpu_fpga_top_bcu_mem.sv`, `constraints/bcu1525_mem_top.tcl`): the four UDIMM cores, the governor,
the BMC's UART, the SYSMON and a JTAG-to-AXI master at BAR0's offsets, no XDMA and no accelerator.
`scripts/memtest_jtag.py` programs it over JTAG and tests each DIMM in turn, cool: GO with that DIMM
alone, its BIOS's log on the UART (`CTRL` UART_SEL picks the core), the read windows under traffic
(`--scan`), the BIST (`--gib`), SAFE; `--warm` heats every DIMM with BIST traffic first (at most
76 C) and tests them warm. A BIOS whose memtest fails goes to the host's calibration with
`--hostcal` (section 11; over JTAG its write leveling takes about 20 minutes a DIMM).

**DDR4-1200.** USPDDRPHY's ISERDESE3s run in component mode, whose CLK takes 1.6 ns at least on
the -2 part (625 MHz). At DDR4-1600 their 800 MHz sys4x failed that pulse width check by 0.350 ns
in every image M build, and on the card the BIOS's read leveling found no window on two of C0's
lanes. The UDIMM core is generated at 150 MHz since (be9d1d1b): MMCM VCO 1200 MHz (300 x 4),
sys4x 600 MHz (+0.066 ns), IDELAYCTRL 400 MHz, CL 9, CWL 9, WR 10 (`targets/bcu1525.toml`
`mem_mts = 1200`). One 64-byte beat a 150 MHz cycle is 9.6 GB/s a DIMM each way. LiteX's own
XCU1525 target runs its DDR4 at 125 MHz.

**The UDIMMs at DDR4-1200 on the card** (2026-10-26, the first card, image M be9d1d1b, each DIMM alone from
a die of 63-65 C). Each DIMM's BIOS: C0's and C2's passed (memtest OK); C1's and C3's failed on one
lane each, C1's lane 6 and C3's lane 1. Their write leveling found a clean edge like the other
lanes', their read leveling no window at any bitslip; every other lane read a window under
traffic of 147-173 taps (a tCK is 484) and the 8 GiB BIST (random and address data) clean, except
one wrong beat on C2's lane 2 in one random pass. `scripts/lane_diag.py` on C1's lane 6: at the
BIOS's settings 765 wrong beats of 262,144 (16 MiB) at the best read point, on all eight bits,
the same count every run, inside regions of the usual window's width at read bitslips 6 and 7;

## 15. The fan: `scripts/fan_pid.py` and its status file

The first card has had a fan on its heatsink since 2026-10-28, on a small fan controller of its
own (`--fanctl` names the library that drives it).
`scripts/fan_pid.py` holds the die at `--target` (60 C here) with it; its docstring has the loop,
the tuning and the failsafes. It runs in a terminal multiplexer session on the card host and is
the card's one poller there: every `--interval` (2 s) it reads the card, sets the duty, prints
the live line, appends a row to `--csv` (its last columns `pin_w` and `vin`; a file begun by an
older fan_pid keeps its columns) and replaces the status file.

**Two fans** (fan_pid 1.2, 2026-10-31). The second card has a fan on the controller's second
output (duty only). fan_pid runs a loop per card in the one process that holds the controller's
port: fan 1 by the first card (its JTAG serial `--serial`, its PCI function `--pci`), fan 2 by
the second (`--serial2`, `--pci2`). Their defaults are `BCU_CARD0_SERIAL` / `_BDF` and
`BCU_CARD1_SERIAL` / `_BDF` (the environment, or the host's `~/.config/otpu/bcu.env`); the
controller's two-fan client is `boards/bcu1525/scripts/fanctl.py`. With both cards on the bus `/dev/xdma0` can be either, so each card's device is
the xdma node under its PCI function, looked up at each reading (`--dev` / `--dev2`: a device as
given). Each card has its own reading (over JTAG each its own Vivado on the one hw_server), loop,
overrides, blind timeout, status file (fan 2's `--status2`) and CSV (`--csv2`, default `--csv`'s
name with a 2: `fan.csv` -> `fan2.csv`); the gains, limits and source are both cards';
`--target2` is fan 2's die target (default `--target`). `--tune` tunes fan 1's loop. `--no-fan2`,
or a fan tool or firmware without fan 2 (a warning at the start), drives fan 1 alone, as 1.1
did. The lines are then kept lines named by card (no live line). An error sets both fans 100 %
and leaves the controller's watchdog on (1.1 turned it off).

What it reads over PCIe (`--source pcie`, or `auto` while the governor answers), all of it read
only: the governor's registers; the SYSMON's temperature register only once the governor's die
is gone (section 13, "The SYSMON's registers"); and, with the BMC's watch (the governor's
HAS_REG: a BMC2 bridge), the watch's registers through `bmc.watch` over the same mapping: the
bridge's ID, W_HOT, W_P0 to W_VIN, W_FW and W_CNT. Never the bridge's STATUS (its read clears
the error flags) or RX (its read pops a byte), and no BMC session. Over JTAG (`--source jtag`, or
`auto` without the governor) it has the die alone.

**The status file** (`--status FILE`; `--status ''` for none) is JSON, written whole every step: a temporary file beside it renamed over it, so a reader
sees the last step's file or the one before, never part of one. A monitor (a top bar on another
host, say) polls it with

    ssh <card host> cat <status file>

and judges its age by `time` against its own clock: the file changes every `interval_s`, so one
more than about 10 s old means fan_pid has stopped or hangs (the fan controller's watchdog sets
100 % 15 s after its last command). A file that stops changing keeps its last values; nothing
else marks it. Missing readings are `null`.

| Field | What |
|---|---|
| `time`, `iso` | the step's time: Unix seconds, and ISO 8601 with the card host's offset |
| `interval_s` | seconds between steps (`--interval`) |
| `source` | `pcie` or `jtag` (the source the step read) |
| `state.mode` | `RUN` or `SAFE` (the governor's STATUS); `null` over JTAG |
| `state.dimms`, `state.acc` | the DIMMs on (0-3) and the accelerator on |
| `state.text` | the live line's state: `SAFE C0 C2`, with `gov frozen` / `sysmon frozen` |
| `die_c` | the die the loop follows: the governor's while it moves, else the SYSMON register's, or the JTAG reading; `null` when every reading is frozen (then the duty is 100 %) |
| `gov_c`, `sysmon_c` | the governor's die (`null` once frozen or not valid) and the SYSMON register's (read only then) |
| `gov_frozen`, `sysmon_frozen` | each die reading's frozen verdict (the governor's own TEMP_AGE, or 60 s of the same code) |
| `gov_age_s` | the governor's TEMP_AGE: seconds since its code last changed (`null` on builds without it) |
| `reg_c` | the VCCINT regulator's hottest temperature (the governor's REG, `null` once 10 s old) |
| `power.vccint_in_w` | the VCCINT regulator's input power, READ_PIN from the BMC's watch (W) |
| `power.vin_v` | its input voltage, READ_VIN (V) |
| `power.age_s`, `power.stale` | the power reading's age, and whether it is older than the governor's REG_STALE (10 s) |
| `power.label` | `VCCINT regulator input (READ_PIN), not the board total` |
| `fan.duty`, `fan.why` | the duty set (%), and why when the loop did not choose it (an override at a limit, a hold or 100 % without a reading); `null` while the loop regulates |
| `fan.mode`, `fan.target_c`, `fan.filt_c`, `fan.lead` | `pid` or `tune`, the die's target, the filtered control temperature, which reading leads it (`die` or `vreg`) |
| `fan_pid.pid`, `.version`, `.tree`, `.started` | the process, fan_pid's version, the tree it runs from (`tree-<sha8>`), its start (Unix seconds) |
| `fan.gains`, `fan.i` | the gains in effect (`kp`, `ki`, `kd`) and the integral (% duty) |
| `card`, `fan_no` | the card's name with two fans (`<card> fan 1`, `<card> fan 2`; `null` with one), and the fan (1 or 2) |

**The gains while it runs.** Each step the loop reads `gains_live.json` beside the status file
(`{"fan1": {"kp": .., "ki": .., "kd": .., "target_c": ..}, "fan2": ...}`, `target_c` optional)
and puts this fan's on its PID, the integral kept, so that a new ki does not bump the duty. The
dashboard's Fan control panel writes it (`POST /api/fan_gains`, docs/dashboard.md). What is in it
wins over `--kp`, `--ki`, `--kd` and `--target`, also after a restart: the line `gains of fan N
now ...` in the pane says so; delete the file for the flags at the next start. Numbers above kp
100, ki 10 or kd 1000, or a target outside 40-70 C, are refused and the gains stay. The target is
the die's: the regulator keeps its own (`--reg-target`, default the start's target + 10 C), and
whichever is further above its own still leads.
The overrides (`--hot-c`, `--reg-hot-c`) do not depend on the gains. `--tune` ignores the file.

The power is VCCINT's alone: the LTC3884's input on the 12 V side, its own loss included
(section 13), not the DIMMs, the transceivers or the card's other rails. Idle in SAFE it reads
about 2-3 W (2.0 W at a 40 C die, 2.9 W at 76 C: leakage).
