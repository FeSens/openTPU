# Two cards in tensor parallel (bcu1525-x2)

Two BCU1525s, two slices each, run one model as a four-slice machine: 64 GiB of DDR4, 32 channels,
153.6 GB/s of DRAM at 150 MHz. The slices' collectives cross between the cards over two QSFP28
cables (docs/x2_link.md). This page is the system: how a model is split, what the hub and the host
do, how it is simulated, and what the cards measured. Labels: **card** is a run on the two cards,
**co-sim** the two-board RTL simulation, **ISA** the instruction-set simulator, **estimate**
arithmetic.

## Summary

- **One compile, one bitstream.** The compiler sees S = 4 (`targets/bcu1525-x2.toml`). Both cards
  hold the same two-slice bitstream; a register (X2_CARD) says which card is which. Card c runs
  global slices 2c and 2c + 1.
- **Bit-exact (card).** Every qualification run and every cut below ends with instruction
  counts, logits and DRAM bytes equal to the ISA simulator's, the two hubs' collective counts
  equal, and no CRC or soft error on the link. The chats have no ISA reference of the real
  model: they are compared with one card's replies (equal on the short chat, not at 31k: see
  Open).
- **Decode cuts at 150 MHz (card).** Share of both cards' DRAM bandwidth, projected to the full
  model: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B 85.2 % (66.8 tok/s), Qwen3.8-27B 88.4 % (9.30 tok/s), GPT-OSS-20B 86.3 %
  (52.8 tok/s). Tensor parallel over two cards keeps 96-98 % of what one card's slice reaches.
- **The co-simulation predicts the card within 1 %** on all three cuts (not on a tiny model
  whose token is DRAM time: section 6).
- **The 35B's chat (card).** Decode 65.1 tok/s against one card's 33.3 (1.96x); a 2,689-token
  paste at 97.6 prompt tokens a second on the device against 43.0 (2.27x); at a 31k context,
  3.10 Mcycles a token against 7.37.
- **Open.** At a 30,870-token context the 35B's reply on two cards differs from one card's from
  the reply's eighth word on, the same way on the two runs compared (section 8).

## 1. The machine

| | one FPGA | the machine the host and the compiler see |
|---|---|---|
| slices | 2 | 4 |
| DRAM | 32 GiB, 16 channels | 64 GiB, 32 channels |
| CFG_PLATFORM | 7 | 7 |
| CFG_HASH | E331F79C | D5239726 |
| core clock | each card's own, 150 MHz | |

- The target stays `bcu1525-x2:st16` at S = 4. The build derives a card's copy with S = 2 before
  it computes the card's hash (boards/bcu1525/x2/README.md), so a card never carries the
  machine's hash; the host checks each card's, then advertises the machine's to the engine.
- Each slice has its own channels, image and program. Card c's local channel 8j + k is global
  channel 16c + 8j + k. Addresses inside a slice's DRAM are the same on both cards.
- The programs on card 1 differ from card 0's only in the global slice ids they carry. Card 1 is
  not compiled again at S = 2. `tools/x2.py compile POINT` prints a point's programs, IMEM rows
  and image bytes by card.
- On each FPGA slice 0 is in SLR0 and slice 1 in SLR2, each beside two DIMMs; the hub, the
  control block and PCIe are in SLR1; the link is in SLR2 beside the QSFP quads.

## 2. The hub's remote port

At one card the collectives hub (`otpu_coll_hub`, `rtl/top/otpu_coll.sv`) gathers its slices'
rows. With NC = 2 (`otpu_coll_xc`) each card's hub also sends its slices' pieces to the other
card and takes the other's:

- A GATHER's pieces and a BAR's token go out as messages of at most 32 words, numbered by the
  collective's sequence (docs/x2_link.md section 3). At two cards either link carries them.
- **Hold.** A remote message stays in the link's RX FIFO until the local slices have issued the
  same collective. So the cards need no common start: the first collective absorbs the skew of
  the two RUN writes, and RUN = 0 never resets the link.
- **Order.** Remote pieces may arrive in any order; a header of another collective than n or
  n + 1, a piece seen twice, or a piece that is not another card's is an error.
- **FOLLOW.** On one card a reader may follow a gathered row as it fills. Across cards only
  local writes advance that front, so a follower could read remote words that have not arrived.
  The host therefore clears FOLLOW in the wait entries it loads on a multi-card machine
  (`Board.load_program`, and the chained generate programs' slots): a reader waits for the whole
  gather. The first real-model run found this: without it the tiny model's logits were wrong.
- **Errors.** A link's check (a CRC, an RX overflow, a dropped channel), a header out of place,
  ABORT from the other card, or the watchdog (COLL_WD: 2^26 core cycles by default, 0.447 s at
  150 MHz, of a collective waiting on the links with no word moving) aborts the collectives:
  ABORT goes out of both links, every word received is dropped, and each collective completes on
  its local writes alone, so the program runs to its HALT and the host sees the cause in
  COLL_STATUS and STATUS ERROR. It stays aborted until a link reset on both stopped cards.
- **X2_SEQ** counts the collectives every local slice issued since the link reset, modulo
  65,536, across runs.

## 3. The host (`opentpu/host/cards.py`)

`CardsTransport` presents the four slices as one transport to `Board`, `BoardBackend` and the
engine (`--dev /dev/xdmaA+/dev/xdmaB`).

- **Routing.** Register writes go to both cards, card 1 then card 0; the two RUN writes are back
  to back, with no barrier. STATUS combines HALTED, RUN, WR_IDLE and calibration with AND and
  ERROR with OR. ICOUNT is routed by global slice; CYCLES and the ordinary counters are card 0's.
  Images and scrubs go to both cards at once, a thread a card.
- **Nothing of the model on the host.** Embedding lookup and token selection run on the cards as
  on one card. The host reads the tokens from card 0.
- **Open.** Both cores stopped and their writes drained; X2_CARD on each card; LRST on both; both
  ports of both cards up within 2 s; the hellos read into a cable map (the magic, the other
  card's id and NCARDS, symmetry: straight and crossed cabling both pass); COLL_WD set.
- **After every run.** Both cards' X2_SEQ must agree, and no COLL_STATUS cause, STATUS ERROR,
  port sticky or port down may show; any of them raises `LinkError` with the card, the port and
  the registers. Where the program's dynamic collective count is known (immediate LOOPs are
  counted without unrolling; a caller can supply it), each card's X2_SEQ must have advanced by
  exactly that count, since equality alone would not see both cards skip an operation.
- **Errors and recovery.** `CardsTransport.recover()` stops both cores, waits for the writes to
  drain, and repeats the link reset and its checks. It never replays a token: a failed run may
  have written part of its KV rows and states, so the caller restores its own checkpoint. The
  chat rolls the turn back to its snapshot and asks again; the servers drop the device's
  contexts and resume their requests from the KV store (docs/serve.md, docs/batch35.md), once a
  request.

## 4. Simulation

| | what it is | what it shows |
|---|---|---|
| `IsaCards` | one ISA machine at S = 4 behind two cards' register and DMA interfaces | the host's routing, card ids, register protocol and image placement, and the engine's results. Its CYCLES read zero |
| `X2SimTransport` over `sim/verilator/tb_x2.sv` | two boards in RTL (each two slices, the memory bridges, the MIG pairs and DDR4 models), both hubs, two `otpu_link_sim` cables at L = 40 cycles | cycles, and bit-exactness of the RTL against the ISA |
| `otpu_link_pair` in place of a cable | the real link endpoints over an Aurora stand-in | the cable model against the RTL (docs/x2_link.md section 6) |

Checked on the co-sim against the S = 4 ISA reference, every fp32 logit bit, argmax, generated
token and per-slice instruction count: a tiny Qwen3 (3 decode steps and 6 generated tokens,
11,296 cycles a token), and the whole 28-layer Qwen3-0.6B (3 decode steps of 727,621-728,537
cycles and 3 generated tokens; 4,815 instructions a slice a step; 1 h 18 min of simulation, 16
GiB). `tests/test_cards.py` holds them (the real checkpoint's is opt-in), `tests/test_coll_x2.py`
the hubs alone: 200-collective random GATHER / BAR streams at latencies 0, 40 and 240, straight
and crossed, with link and run resets.

**Cuts.** Decode cuts time the models without their weights: `fp4:int8` at position 60, four
layers of Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (q36-4) and of Qwen3.8-27B (q38-4) with a 4,096-word vocabulary, two
layers of GPT-OSS-20B (goss-2) with its whole head, zero weights and the routing words seeded the
same in the RTL and the ISA. `tools/x2_cosim.py` runs one and checks every byte below the
image's low end, the logits, and the instruction and collective counts. A full token is
projected from a cut: the prologue once, the measured body 10 / 16 / 12 times, the Qwen heads
estimated (GPT-OSS's is measured). Share of bandwidth is useful bytes / (1,024 B x cycles of a
token); 1,024 B a cycle is both cards' DRAM, 153.6 GB/s at 150 MHz.

## 5. The KV split

Qwen3.6-35B-A3B has 2 KV heads. At S = 4 each is copied on two slices, and before the split every
copy read the head's whole history for its own 4 query heads: 512 B a position a slice, the DRAM
bound of decode at depth (q36-4: 182,598 cycles at position 60, 263,983 at 31,000).

With `KVSPLIT` (a compile choice of the target, like SEXACT; not in the bitstream's hash), a
decode bucket of that many attention blocks or more splits a head's blocks between its two
copies, which sit on the same card:

- **The queries.** Each slice's 4 queries are gathered with every slice's in one GATHER. Each
  copy then attends the pair's 8 queries over its own blocks: copy p takes blocks p, p + 2, ...
- **The combine.** The partner's rows of the partial softmax are exchanged (m and l in one small
  GATHER, then the accumulators), and each slice merges its own rows as flash decoding does:
  m = max(m_a, m_b), w_x = 2^(m_x - m) / (l_a 2^(m_a - m) + l_b 2^(m_b - m)), o = acc_a w_a +
  acc_b w_b.
- **The schedule.** The gate's projection is emitted after the query GATHER, which hides behind
  it, and the weights are computed while the accumulators travel, so two full-width vector
  operations follow the last GATHER.
- **Writes stay on both copies.** Each copy appends every token's K and V, so the cache is whole
  on each. Prefill, the KV store, snapshots and rollback are unchanged.

Co-sim, q36-4, cycles:

| position | unsplit | split | change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 60 | 182,598 | (below the threshold) | 0 |
| 1,000 | 182,713 | 183,560 | +0.46 % |
| 3,000 | 188,096 | 188,387 | +0.15 % |
| 4,000 | 191,549 | 190,944 | -0.32 % |
| 31,000 | 263,983 | 254,777 | -3.49 % |

The split removes only the DRAM term: the DMA's busy share of the attention core halves (35.8 %
to 17.8 %) and the core becomes MXU-bound (the MXU's and VPU's work per query and position is the
same split or not). So the split alone stops near 255K at 31,000; with a wider MXU path on top
(the `wmx` variant's levers) the same point goes from 261,539 to 237,586, -9.2 %. Break-even is
about 2,750 positions, and the threshold is 12 blocks (position 2,816 on). `bcu1525-x2:wmx`
sets it; `bcu1525-x2:st16`, the variant carded here, does not split.

A split bucket changes the softmax's order of summation, so it is not S = 1's result bit for bit
(on a tiny model: cosine above 0.9999). The RTL against the ISA stays bit-exact. Models with
4 or more KV heads (the 27B, GPT-OSS, Qwen3-0.6B, LFM2) are not affected, and prefill's row runs
and MTP never split: prefill reads each K / V block once for a run's rows, so the term the split
removes is already 1/R of decode's.

## 6. On the cards: qualification and the cuts

Two BCU1525s, the image at 150 MHz, DDR4-2400. `tools/x2_card.py` runs the steps.

- **Bring-up.** Each card on its own: calibration, identity, the configuration words, memory.
  Then the four-slice qualification: three BARs and a GATHER bit-exact; Qwen3-0.6B int8 decode
  tokens [1479, 198, 12555] and device generation [374, 279, 897], the co-sim's; X2_SEQ +168 a
  decode; 0 CRC and soft errors.
- **Qwen3-0.6B decode**: 706,497-706,993 cycles, 4.71 ms (at 100 MHz: 478K cycles, 4.78 ms). The
  small model's token is DRAM time, not core cycles.
- **The cuts**, three repetitions each. Before each: the DRAM image cleared and the programs
  loaded from prebuilt packs (no compile, image build or reference on the card host). After
  each: instruction counts, 4,096 logit words, argmax, a SHA-256 of every low-area byte of all
  four slices, the collective counts, clean links.

| cut | cycles (median of 3) | range | co-sim | card against co-sim | full model (projected) | share of two cards' DRAM |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| q36-4 (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) | 181,853 | 181,702-181,925 | 182,598 | -0.41 % | 66.8 tok/s | 85.2 % (130.9 GB/s) |
| q38-4 (Qwen3.8-27B) | 945,116 | 943,062-946,071 | 940,031 | +0.54 % | 9.30 tok/s | 88.4 % (135.8 GB/s) |
| goss-2 (GPT-OSS-20B) | 801,617 | 801,234-801,871 | 809,560 | -0.98 % | 52.8 tok/s | 86.3 % (132.6 GB/s) |

- Against one card at S = 1 the cuts run 2.02x, 1.95x and 1.97x. Those one-card cycles
  (367,844 / 1,839,124 / 1,580,314) are an earlier study's programs, not same-day builds, so the
  ratios are indicative.
- Against one card's measured share of its own DRAM (35B 88.5 %, 27B 90.6 %, GPT-OSS about 88 %),
  two cards keep 96 %, 98 % and 98 % of it.
- The 4-way split costs more than the cable. On the board model before the cards (K7 DDR
  timing, an earlier tree's programs, so ratios only), four slices against one at link latency 0
  took 0.522 of the cycles on q36-4 and 0.511 on goss-2 against an ideal 0.5; raising the
  latency from 0 to 240 cycles added 1.12 % and 0.05 %, and 40 cycles changed neither ratio.
  The link measures 31 cycles port to port, 39 with the budget for the hub's crossings
  (docs/x2_link.md).
- The co-simulation's 1 % is these three cuts'. On Qwen3-0.6B, whose token is DRAM time and not
  core cycles, the card took 706K cycles a token against the co-simulation's 728K (3 % under);
  the 100 MHz image took 478K (34 % under).

**The image.** The 150 MHz run ended at WNS -0.097 ns; a post-route pass (phys_opt
AggressiveExplore, then route Explore) left -0.035 ns on the 300 MHz memory clock, -0.019 ns on
the core clock and one Min Skew pulse-width check failing on a MIG's TX bitslice inputs. It was
carded as a development image, under the build's allowance of -0.1 ns, and its qualification is
the bit-exact checks above. The later sessions ran a clean re-route of the same RTL.

## 7. On the cards: the 35B's chat

Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, fp4 weights with an int8 head, its whole 262,144-token context, the same four
turns on both cards and on one (greedy; the replies equal word for word):

| | both cards | one card |
|---|---|---|
| decode (1,024 tokens) | 65.13 tok/s (device 65.3), 2.30 Mcycles a token | 33.27 tok/s (device 33.3), 4.51 Mcycles |
| short turn's first token (24 prompt tokens) | 1.89 s | 1.15 s |
| a 2,689-token paste, first token | 57.37 s (device 97.6 tok/s) | 70.14 s (device 43.0 tok/s) |
| a 90-token follow-up (2,749 reused) | 4.93 s (device 93.0 tok/s) | 8.57 s (device 41.3 tok/s) |
| load | 160.6 s | 32.5 s |

Decode is 1.96x one card's and the paste's device rate 2.27x. The paste's first token gains
less (1.22x): the time outside the device's runs grew from about 7.6 s to about 29.8 s, not
broken down on this session (at S > 1 the layer runs come through the program cache, a program
a slice, with no compile workers). A later session with the cache's words handed to the board
as they are (below) read 46.5 s for the same paste. The load is the image's 19.6 GB read from a hard disk at ~181 MB/s; the write goes to
both cards at once.

Decode's 2.30 Mcycles is 85 % of both cards' DRAM by the token's bytes (499 MB a slice).

**At a 30,870-token context:**

| | both cards | one card |
|---|---|---|
| the prompt | first token 668.5 s (device 78.5 tok/s) | 1,033.7 s (device 31.1 tok/s) |
| decode at 31k | 36.3 tok/s (device 48.4), 3.10 Mcycles a token | 19.6 tok/s (device 20.4), 7.37 Mcycles |
| a follow-up (30,866 reused) | first token 3.68 s | 12.94 s |

About 275 s of the prompt's 668.5 s are outside the device: 67 s reading 4,989 layer runs from
the program cache and decoding them into instructions, 117 s in the 5,047 starts that assemble
them again for the board, and the rest the per-run path of about 617k runs. Handing the
cache's words to the board as they are (`opentpu.host.runwords`: the same programs word for
word) cut the first token to 564 s on a later session, with the replies unchanged.

**Two bugs the cards found**, both with tests that fail without the fix
(`tests/test_chat_x2.py`): a warm that cached only slice 0's decode program at S > 1, so the
other slices ran HALT and the first step never ended (the board now refuses fewer programs than
slices); and garbage from position 256 on, the first HALT CHAIN into the next generate bucket,
whose wait entries had kept FOLLOW (section 2).

## 8. Determinism, and the open issue

- The 31k session run twice on the two cards gave the same replies word for word (one repeat):
  no race between the cards showed. The short chat equals one card's. A tiny model shaped as
  the 35B (16 query and 2 KV heads, a DeltaNet and an attention layer, 8 experts top 2) along a
  30,870-token prompt gives the same logits bit for bit on the ISA at S = 1, at S = 4, through
  the cards' host path (`IsaCards`), and on the two cards (21 of 21 comparisons).
- **Open: the real 35B at 31k.** The prompt and the first seven words of the first reply are one
  card's. At word 8 (about decode token 9, position ~30,880, inside one generate bucket) the two
  cards say "the details regarding the vault and the guild's seal" where one card says "the
  answers to your questions". Both replies go on to the right facts. The difference is the same
  on the runs compared (the repeat above and the session with `runwords`), so it is not a race
  and not an intermittent link error; the tiny probe above is exact at S = 4, so it is not the split's arithmetic in general. It has not been localized: the
  next step is the first differing logit position against an ISA reference of the real model (a
  shortened one, or a mid-size proxy).
- The cut's share for the 35B, 85.2 %, is under the 86 % the one-card image was built for. The
  board model puts the 35B's cost of the 4-way split in its routing-fed gathers; that has not
  been measured on the cards.
