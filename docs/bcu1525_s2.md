# The BCU1525 at two slices: a slice a die, two DIMMs each

`targets/bcu1525-s2.toml`: two WG 8 slices on one card, each on a die of its own with two
DDR4-2400 DIMMs, the shell on the die between. It is also the design each card runs in the
two-card machine (`bcu1525-x2`, section 6). This page is what two slices change against one
([bcu1525_wg.md](bcu1525_wg.md)), the floorplans, what the split gives decode and prefill, where
pipelining helps and where it does not, and the card's results.

**Short answer.**
- **Two slices do not make one card's decode faster.** The same four DIMMs feed them; one slice
  already drains all four at twice their rate. On the card at 150 MHz the three models' decode
  cuts take from 2.1% fewer to 0.4% more cycles at S = 2 than at S = 1, and decode loops on
  zero-weight images read 89.4% (35B), 90.3% (27B) and 87.4% (GPT-OSS) of the DIMMs' peak.
  There was no real-weights token check at S = 2.
- **Prefill is where the second MXU pays**: a 512-token prompt's time to first token 1.57x on the
  27B and 1.46x on the 35B (board model), growing with the rows a pass.
- **The split is exact.** With SEXACT the S = 2 programs give S = 1's results bit for bit, so
  there is one reference set.
- **At S = 1 the lever was the sequencer's window, not a weight prefetch.** A window of 32 with
  saturating wait counts took the 35B from 84.97% to 86.01%; a full scoreboard was exact but
  about 25K LUTs too dear.
- **Plan c is the floorplan**: slice 0 in SLR0 on C0 + C1, slice 1 in SLR2 on C2 + C3, the shell
  in SLR1. Plans with a slice in SLR1 fill it to 87% of its LUTs.
- **Two cards over QSFP28** (four slices, 153.6 GB/s of DIMMs): cuts bit-exact on the cards,
  projecting to 85-88% of the two cards' peak for the full models. One open difference at 31k
  context (section 6).

## 1. What two slices change, and what they do not

The card's four DIMMs move 76.8 GB/s at peak at one slice or two. At S = 1 the slice's bridges
take 8 x 32 bytes at 300 MHz = 76.8 GB/s and port W 153.6 GB/s at 150 MHz. At S = 2 each slice
owns two DIMMs, 38.4 GB/s: twice its bridges' headroom, four times its port W's. So:

- **Decode** is bandwidth-bound: it gains at most the phases that do not overlap the stream (the
  VPU's, attention's, the DeltaNet steps), which take half the time on half the work, and it
  pays the collectives (a GATHER moves a slice's half of each projection's output to the other).
- **Prefill** is MXU-bound at 4-8 rows a pass: two full MXUs, until a slice's two DIMMs bind.
- **The floorplan gets easier.** One slice was 56% of SLR2's LUTs; two slices are each the same
  share of a die, and the shell has SLR1 to itself.

**The target.** S 2, each slice bcu1525-wg's (D 128, MCOLS 8, WG 8), 16 GiB a slice. A slice's 8
channels are four a DIMM, interleaved at 4 KiB (`otpu_mig_pair` NC 4): board channel g = 8 s + j
is on DIMM slot g >> 2 at byte `{o[30:12], g & 3, o[11:0]}`, a channel 2 GiB. On by default: the
bank-half window (BHW 33), MM's K tail, the sequencer's window of 32, SEXACT, the pipelined
collective hub (COLL_PIPE) with two register stages each way between a slice and the shell
(XRS 2). Variant `st16` adds the 16-bit DeltaNet states.

**SEXACT.** S = 1's DeltaNet head groups and dense MLP chunks are laid over the slices so that
every sum is taken in S = 1's order. The S = 2 step is then S = 1's bit for bit, and on the 35B's
2-layer cut on the board model it is also 0.62% faster than the free split (its GATHERs 13.2k
-> 7.8k cycles). It is a compile
choice, not in the CFG_HASH.

## 2. Floorplans

DIMM C0's pins are in SLR0, C1's and C2's in SLR1 with PCIe, C3's in SLR2.
`scripts/bcu_fp.py` holds three plans (`WG_PLAN`), each giving the slices' SLRs, the DIMM slots'
map (DMAP) and each channel's bridge pipeline (RLY: 2 stages on the slice's die, 5 one crossing
away, 9 two):

| Plan | Slice 0 | Slice 1 | DMAP | RLY (board channels 15 .. 0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a | SLR1, C1 + C2 (local) | SLR2, C3 (local) + C0 (2 crossings) | 0x39 | 0x9999222222222222 |
| b | SLR1, C0 (1) + C1 (local) | SLR2, C2 (1) + C3 (local) | 0xE4 | 0x2222555522225555 |
| **c** | SLR0, C0 (local) + C1 (1) | SLR2, C2 (1) + C3 (local) | 0xE4 | 0x2222555555552222 |

Estimated LUTs a die, from the S = 1 build's measured parts (a slice with its adapter and 8
bridge core halves about 231K; XDMA 55K; a MIG about 13K; the DMA's interconnect 15K; the same
sum for the S = 1 build's SLR2 is within 10% of its measured 221K):

| Plan | SLR0 | SLR1 | SLR2 | A slice die's DSP / URAM / BRAM |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| a, b | 28.8K (7%) | **341.3K (87%)** | 230.9K (59%) | 66% / 49% / 45% |
| c | 230.9K (59%) | 139.3K (35%) | 230.9K (59%) | 66% / 49% / 45% |

a and b put a slice beside XDMA and two MIGs; one slice there was 94.8% of SLR1's CLBs and failed
timing ([bcu1525_wg.md](bcu1525_wg.md) section 4). c gives each slice a die that looks like the
S = 1 build's SLR2 and leaves SLR1 at 35%. Crossings under c are about 4,000 signals at each
boundary (23-27% of the SLLs), under the S = 1 build's routed 28.7%.

**As built** (the two-card image at 150 MHz, this design with the link port, placed): the slice
SLRs hold 61.9% / 61.5% of their LUTs and 93.6% / 94.8% of their CLB sites; a slice is 179,689
LUTs. At that density 150 MHz is at the edge (section 6: that image's first route ended at WNS
-0.035 ns); an experimental wider-MXU variant, at 98% of the sites, missed 100 MHz
([bcu1525_wg.md](bcu1525_wg.md) section 6).

Each slice keeps its full MXU (66% of a die's DSPs). Half an MXU would still feed a slice's two
DIMMs twice over, but prefill is MXU-bound and would give back the doubling.

## 3. Building it

    cd boards/bcu1525
    IMAGE=wg WG_TARGET=bcu1525-s2:st16 WG_PLAN=c WG_FP=soft CORE_MHZ=150 FAST=0 \
      WG_MIGMAP=b15 OTPU_IP_CACHE= JOBS=1 SYNTH_THREADS=1 STEP=synth ./run_vivado.sh

then `STEP=impl`, as at one slice. Two-slice knobs of `run_vivado.sh`:

| Knob | What |
|---|---|
| `WG_PLAN=a\|b\|c` | the floorplan (default c) |
| `WG_MXU_COLS0`, `WG_MXU_COLS1` | each slice's MXU DSP columns |
| `WG_MMCM` | the clock wizard's MMCM site (default MMCM_X1Y5; `none`: the placer's) |
| `WG_STOP=synth\|link` | stop after synthesis's checks, or after the implementation's link: a fit check without place and route |
| `WG_UMEM=1`, `WG_CPORT=1` | the adapters and bridge core halves, or each slice's collective port, moved into SLR1 |
| `WG_MIGKO`, `WG_MIGKO_W` | an exclusion pblock beside each DIMM's PHY, its width in SLICE columns (default 12) |
| `WG_PRS`, `WG_HRS` | AXI register slices on the DIMMs' ports (on by default at two slices) and on the host's DMA to C0 and C3 |

Place and route are expected to peak near 28-31 GB (an estimate; the first two-slice placement
was killed at 26.9 GB resident on a 32 GB host), so one job and one synthesis thread there.

**What 150 MHz took at two slices.** The first image (100 MHz, development strategies) ran on the
card and missed timing by 0.332 ns over 2,857 endpoints, every failing path 90-98% route:

| Clock | WNS / endpoints | Where | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| aclk (3.332 ns) | -0.238 / 2,047 | a bridge's hold and FIFO pointers (fanout 170-520) into LUT RAMs 100-200 rows away; pipeline stages placed across the die boundary | register copies per column group; each pipeline stage assigned to a die |
| a MIG's ui_clk | -0.202, -0.332 | inside the outer dies' MIGs, the slice on the same die | none (they close at full effort); later the exclusion pblock |
| XDMA's clock | -0.067 / 134 | the DMA's interconnect split over two dies | it goes in SLR1's pblock |
| core_clk to the 2x clock | -0.027 / 1 | 1.87 ns of skew: the MMCM in SLR0, its input over 2.39 ns of backbone | the MMCM at MMCM_X1Y5, by its input |

The copies are cycle for cycle the same RTL (the bridge's CPY, the port mux's RCP: read selects
and write pointers in copies, each over a part of the data), on at S = 2 only.

The first 150 MHz build then met setup and failed hold only: WHS -0.379 ns on 7 endpoints, all
from the accelerator's reset in core_clk into bridge logic in acc_clk on the outer dies, with
0.72-1.65 ns of clock skew between the dies. Hold-fixing directives made setup worse (-2.759 ns).
The fix is in the RTL: the reset's last two stages are in acc_clk, and core_clk meets acc_clk
once, between two registers the floorplan pins together in SLR1 beside the clock's root; copies
of the last stage reach the slices in acc_clk alone. The build with it: WNS 0.000, WHS +0.006
ns.

## 4. Pipelining: where the stream stops, and what a prefetch could give

The MXU pops a 1 KiB chunk a cycle, twice the four DIMMs' rate at S = 1. A decode token loses
time only where the weight stream stops or runs slow. On the board model at S = 1, before the
levers below:

| Cut | Share of 76.8 GB/s | Stream idle | Where |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 27B (3 DeltaNet + 1 attention layer) | 87.1% | 14,672 of 1,920,850 cycles | DeltaNet layers at 64% (the state's traffic beside the stream), MLP 85.6%, attention 81.1% |
| 35B-A3B (4 layers) | 76.9% | 29,433 of 414,135 | DeltaNet 44.2%, experts 92.3%; 20.8k cycles between the router and the first expert |
| GPT-OSS-20B (2 layers) | 83.2% | 2,259 of 353,211 | experts 81.0%, attention 66.3% (the MXU idle 35%), head 96.2% |

Two kinds of loss. *Stops*: no chunk request outstanding; the 35B's router-to-expert gap is the
largest, the sequencer's window of 16 full of stores and loads so that the first expert's MM is
not yet dispatched. *Slow streams*: requests outstanding but under the DIMMs' rate, most of the
27B's loss. Long streams reach 92-96%, so the controllers are not the ceiling. The window was
full 97% and 92% of the cycles in the 27B's and the 35B's traces.

**A stream oracle** bounds any prefetch before designing one: simulation only, the MXU records
each MM's stream and replays the list early, each entry behind a gate. Rule *dep*: an MM whose
weights' address comes from an RLD (experts, gathered rows), or whose operand is written this
step, waits for its own accept; every other MM streams as early as the chunk FIFO has room.

| S = 1, FIFO | today | 1 MiB | 2 MiB | 4 MiB | 8 MiB |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 35B-A3B | 76.9% | 77.3% | 78.1% | 79.2% | 79.0% |
| 27B | 87.1% | 86.9% | 87.2% | 87.6% | |
| GPT-OSS-20B | 83.2% | 82.9% | 84.1% | 84.5% | 84.5% |

The 35B's 2.3 points are its DeltaNet layers' static weights streaming ahead of the DSTEPs; the
router-to-expert gap stays, since a FIFO streams in order and static weights queued behind an
RLD-addressed expert cannot pass it. GPT-OSS's gain is all in attention (the next projections
streaming while the attention core runs on the VPU).

**The window was the cheaper lever.** On a later tree, cut cycles against today's:

| | Accept decoupled from issue | Scoreboard, window 16 | Scoreboard, window 32 | Oracle, 4 MiB |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 35B-A3B | -0.70% | -0.60% | -1.85% | -4.21% |
| 27B | -0.14% | | -0.16% | |
| GPT-OSS-20B | +0.07% | -1.67% | -2.66% | |

A footprint scoreboard at a window of 32 is exact but about 25K LUTs with 6.85-7.7 ns of logic:
not built. **Wait counts at a window of 32** (the compiler's 4-bit counts saturating at 15) cost
5,923 LUTs and 3,330 flip-flops with no deeper logic and give most of it:

| Board model, S = 1 | Window 16 | Window 32 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 35B-A3B | 84.97% | 86.01% |
| 27B | 89.61% | 89.70% |
| GPT-OSS-20B | 86.99% | 88.16% |

That, the bank-half window and MM's K tail are `bcu1525-wg:bhw`, on by default here.

**Not built: a weight-prefetch descriptor queue** (the compiler naming, ahead of time, the
static weights a later MM will read, the MXU streaming them into a deeper FIFO). The oracle's
bound for it was up to 4.2% of the 35B's cut before the window and the bank-half window, its
overlap with them not measured; its cost is the FIFO (4 MiB: about 96 RAMB36 or 24 UltraRAMs).
It is the next lever if the 35B needs one.

**What does not help at two slices.**
- *The pipelined hub is free already.* The 27B's tensor-parallel step is within 0.07% of the
  same step without its collectives.
- *A layer pipeline between the slices* (slice 0 the first half of the layers, slice 1 the rest)
  halves decode's bandwidth for one user, loses to batching for two, and for prefill needs whole
  layers on one slice's DIMMs, a second image layout that does not fit beside decode's.
- *An XOR hash over the bank bits* opened more rows at every point (+1.1% to +2.8%): the images'
  layout already keeps the streams that run together on different banks.

## 5. Results

**Board model, decode, S = 2 against S = 1** (after the bank-half window):

| Cut | S = 1 cycles | S = 2 cycles | S = 2 against S = 1 | Share of peak, S = 1 / S = 2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 27B, 4 layers | 1,856,444 | 1,878,225 | -1.17% | 89.9 / 88.9% |
| 35B-A3B, 4 layers | 366,930 | 364,769 | +0.59% | 85.0 / 84.6% |
| GPT-OSS-20B, 2 layers | 1,592,060 | 1,598,810 | -0.42% | 87.1 / 86.5% |

Before the window S = 2 gained 3.7% on the 35B's cut and 1.5% on the 27B's: it had been sidestepping
S = 1's bank conflicts, which the window removed at both. What is left against S = 2: with four
channels a DIMM a read opens 15-21% more rows (the 27B: 46.4 -> 38.5 reads an activate), and the
35B's DeltaNet state chain runs at 13.7 cycles a row on a slice's two DIMMs against 8.0 at S = 1.

**Board model, prefill, a 512-token prompt's time to first token** (estimates from measured
tiles, 150 MHz):

| Model | S = 1 | S = 2 tensor-parallel |
|---|---:|---:|
| 27B (4 rows a pass) | 47.10 s | 30.01 s (1.57x) |
| 35B-A3B (8 rows a pass) | 10.75 s | 7.39 s (1.455x): DeltaNet 1.63x, attention 1.50x, experts 1.26x |

Not 2x: at 4 rows a pass the 27B's half-width tile is already near a slice's two DIMMs (one
slice at full width on two DIMMs takes 1.254x S = 1's time), so the gain grows with the rows a
pass; the experts' fixed costs a pass do not halve.

**On the card.**
- 2026-11-01, the first image (100 MHz, plan c): bring-up, diag, the two-slice selftest ("2
  slices at once" kernels, vector ops, stream) pass; Qwen3-0.6B token for token at 0.93 Mcycles
  a token.
- 2026-11-02, `bcu1525-s2:st16` at 150 MHz (WNS 0.000, WHS +0.006 ns): diag, memory, the
  two-slice selftest pass. Decode cuts' cycles against the S = 1 `:bhw16` image on the card
  (the sign here is the cycles': minus is faster, the opposite of the table above): 35B -2.1%
  (344,641 cycles), 27B +0.4% (1,851,127), GPT-OSS 0.0% (1,590,018). Decode loops on
  zero-weight images: 35B 4,275,436 cycles a token = 89.4%, 27B 31,585,706 = 90.3%, GPT-OSS
  5,606,744 = 87.4%. These are the session's logged numbers; real weights were not run at S = 2.

So two slices are a wash against one on single-stream decode, and their value is prefill, more
than one user, and more than one card.

## 6. Two cards: `bcu1525-x2`

Two cards of this design joined by two QSFP28 cables are a four-slice tensor-parallel machine:
eight DIMMs, 153.6 GB/s, 64 GiB. Global slices 2c and 2c + 1 are on card c; the compiler sees
S = 4 and each card loads its two slices' programs and DRAM shards
(`opentpu.host.cards`: one transport over the cards' own, `/dev/xdma0+/dev/xdma1`). Each card's
collective hub has a link port (`boards/bcu1525/x2/`: Aurora 64B/66B, 4 lanes a cable); a remote
piece of a GATHER is held until the local one issues, with credits, a CRC and a sequence count.

- **The link** (2026-11-02): 25.78125 Gb/s a lane over 0.5 m passive cables; 16 lanes with no
  error in 7.84e12 bits each; a 10-minute soak on all four links with no error; 11.1 GB/s of
  payload a cable and direction; 205-208 ns port to port (31 core cycles at 150 MHz; about 39
  budgeted hub to hub). The hub moves 4.8 GB/s at most, so the link is not a
  bound.
- **Board model** (an earlier tree's programs, so ratios only): four slices against one card's
  one slice, decode 1.917x (35B), 1.956x (GPT-OSS), 1.964x (27B): 96-98% of the second card's
  DIMMs, with no cost at a link latency of 40 cycles. The two-board RTL co-simulation is within
  2% of that and bit-exact.
- **On the cards** (2026-11-03, `bcu1525-x2:st16`): the 100 MHz image passed BARs, GATHERs and
  Qwen3-0.6B decode and generate bit-exact with no CRC or soft error. At 150 MHz the three cuts
  are bit-exact in three runs each: 35B 181,853 cycles, 27B 945,116, GPT-OSS 801,617 (the
  co-simulation within 1%), which project to 66.8 / 9.30 / 52.8 tok/s: **85.2% / 88.4% / 86.3%
  of the two cards' 153.6 GB/s**.
- **As a chat** (the 35B on both cards): decode 65.3 tok/s on the device (about 83% of the
  DIMMs), prefill 97.6 tok/s on a 2,689-token prompt; at 31k context prefill 78.5 tok/s and
  decode 3.10 Mcycles a token. The 31k session repeated once gave the same replies; the short
  chat equals one card's, the 31k one does not (below).

**Open.**
- The real 35B's reply at 31k context on two cards differs from one card's, the same way on
  the runs compared. A tiny model is exact at S = 4 on the cards against the ISA simulator; the
  difference has not been localized (the first differing logit is the next step).
- The 35B at 85.2% is under the 86% goal on two cards.
- `x2/build_x2.sh` builds the 100 MHz functional image only; the 150 MHz image was built with
  `run_vivado.sh` and the directives of [bcu1525_wg.md](bcu1525_wg.md) section 5, and its first
  routed version carried a small negative slack (WNS -0.035 ns on the 300 MHz memory clock) and
  one failing pulse-width check (-0.173 ns, Min Skew on a MIG's TX bitslice inputs; the build's
  gate allows 0.1 ns of setup but has no allowance for pulse width): a development image. A
  clean re-route followed.
